## What "Club Fleece" actually refers to

Nike sells a lot of things called fleece. Club Fleece is the entry-level line: brushed-back fleece, standard fit, embroidered logo on the chest or sleeve, no performance claims. The fabric is usually 80–82% cotton and 18–20% polyester, and Nike's own product pages note that the percentages vary by factory, so the label on your specific item is the one that counts.

Smooth on the outside and brushed soft on the inside is how Nike describes it, and that's a fair summary. It's a layering piece. It is not a jacket, not water resistant, and not the same thing as Club Tech Fleece, which is a separate line with a different fabric and its own price. If you want the slicker finish, that's the one to compare against; both product pages will show you the current numbers side by side.

The line runs across men's, women's and kids' sizing, and the kids' version is worth knowing about separately, because that's where the sizing advice is most blunt.

## Sizing: where Club Fleece catches people out

Nike labels most of this line "standard fit: easy and traditional." That's the marketing version. The product pages themselves carry the more useful warnings:

- The **women's crew-neck** page says plainly: "Fits small; we recommend ordering a size up."
- The **older kids' pullover** carries the same note, and the South African reviews back it up: "I find that they do run a little smaller, we always size up one for extra room."
- The **men's full-zip** is a different story. On the UK page, 53% of reviewers landed between "runs small" and "runs big," which is the middle of the scale, so most people found it about right. But individual reviews mention inconsistency across Nike hoodies generally, with one person noting their usual XL looked huge in this cut.

The honest summary: the men's full-zip is closest to true to size, the women's and kids' cuts run small enough that Nike itself tells you to size up, and if you're between sizes on a men's piece, the reviews aren't going to settle it for you.

## What the reviews keep saying, in both directions

The aggregate numbers on these pages are high: 4.8 stars across 1,911 reviews on the men's crew, 4.8 across 796 on the UK full-zip, 4.7 on the EU version. That's a lot of people who bought a second one.

The recurring complaint is worth knowing about before you commit. On the full-zip, a reviewer who had the piece for five months reported the sleeves beginning to fray, and mentioned air-drying to try to make it last. That's one review, not a pattern, and it sits among hundreds of five-star ones, but it's the kind of thing that shows up repeatedly on brushed fleece at this price point rather than at the Tech Fleece level. Treat Club Fleece as a two-to-three-season layer you'll wear hard, not an heirloom.

On comfort, the numbers are harder to argue with: 93% of UK full-zip reviewers rated it between "uncomfortable" and "very comfortable," clustered toward the comfortable end. It's soft. Nobody's disputing the softness.

## How Club Fleece pricing actually moves

Full price on Nike.com and full price in a Nike store are the same number. There's no channel discount for buying direct, which surprises people who assume the website undercuts retail.

What does move the price:

**Nike Membership.** It's free to join, and on the US site it unlocks free standard shipping on orders of $50 or more plus 60-day returns instead of 30. In the UK and EU the threshold works differently: standard delivery runs £4.50 or €5 unless you're a member above £50/€50, or a non-member above £99. On a single $60 crew, membership is basically the difference between paying for shipping or not.

**End-of-season clearance.** The men's crew being marked down with an extra-code offer on the US page is the normal pattern for this line. Club Fleece gets discounted as the season turns, and the departure from list price is real, not $5 off.
